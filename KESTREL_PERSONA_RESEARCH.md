# Kestrel Air customer portfolio and persona prototypes

**Prepared 24 October 2026.** Kestrel is fictional; the public airline evidence below supplies behavioral mechanisms, not Kestrel customer counts.

## 1. What Kestrel's own dossier establishes

Kestrel is a low-cost US airline with 11 million annual passengers, 36 same-family aircraft, two bases (Orlando and Philadelphia), 75% domestic flying and 25% Mexico/Caribbean flying. Six in ten flights touch a base. The dossier describes price-conscious families visiting relatives, students, vacationers, some small business travelers, and travel agencies booking groups. Many customers fly Kestrel about twice a year and use other airlines too. Week 8 is 97% booked; a planned two-day strike at both bases threatens baggage, cleaning, wheelchair assistance, and fueling. The simulation must measure disruption handling, complaints, repeat intent, and revenue, while respecting the dossier's actions, budget, and DOT refund baseline. [Kestrel dossier](13-kestrel-air.md), [challenge](yeni-case-5-sirket/02-challenge.md).

This implies five useful groups. **Trip purpose is only the starting point.** Within every group, vary the fixedness of arrival time, party size, checked bags, budget buffer, contact channel, other-airline options, and past experience. Those differences can change a decision during the strike.

## 3. Proposed portfolio: 24 prototypes

The companion file [personas_prototype.jsonl](personas_prototype.jsonl) has **5 family visitors, 5 students, 5 vacationers, 5 business travelers, and 4 travel-agency coordinators**. Every group meets the challenge minimum. This near-even allocation is for comparing mechanisms and gives the smaller business and agency groups enough cases for the board to question. **It is not a market share estimate.** Keep these 24 IDs and fixed attributes identical across all five runs.

The profiles span: direct and agency booking; app, phone, and email contact; high and low trip flexibility; domestic and Mexico/Caribbean travel; checked and cabin-only baggage; parties of one and groups; base-touching and non-base routes; students with and without family financial support; travelers with assistance needs; and loyalty enthusiasts versus infrequent flyers. Route labels are scenario examples, since the dossier provides no city-level route list.

## 4. How to use the prototypes in the simulation

1. **Weekly prompt:** give each persona their fixed record, current reservation/trip state, what they actually heard, operational constraints, and the exact eligible Kestrel message. Ask what they do, why, how they feel, and whether they would book again. The LLM must choose the action and report the dossier's allowed weekly status.
2. **Separate exposure from preference:** the world model determines whether a flight touches Orlando/Philadelphia, whether it is on the strike dates, and which recovery seats or support channels are available. The persona determines whether the offer is acceptable. Do not give every persona the same disruption.
3. **Agency unit:** the agency persona is a coordinator for a stated group size. Have the model report the **group booking's dominant travel status** using the same four allowed Kestrel status labels, plus group-level counts if needed. One coordinator is not one passenger in revenue calculations. Record this convention in `TEAM_DOC.md`.
4. **No arbitrary weights yet:** 24 evenly allocated prototypes are a stress-test panel. Set passenger weights only when there is an explicit Kestrel segment distribution or state a sensitivity range; do not claim the panel represents 210,000 weekly customers. The board metrics for flights operated and 24-hour resolution also require operational inputs, not just persona answers.
5. **Contrast experiments:** compare fast rerouting / backup handling with credits / points for fixed versus flexible trips; run with the same persona file and different seeds. Inspect disagreement by group and by baggage, channel, and base exposure.

For the US scenario, DOT refunds for canceled or significantly changed flights are mandatory when the passenger does not accept the offered alternative. The DOT service dashboard distinguishes each airline's voluntary commitments; Kestrel's own dossier defines its business-as-usual benefits. Keep legally required refunds outside the decision menu. [DOT refunds](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), [DOT disruption dashboard](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-cancellation-delay-dashboard).

## 5. What would make the portfolio representative

Ask the fictional client for **booking purpose or proxy**, party size, route and base exposure, booking lead time, fare/ancillary spend, direct versus agency channel, checked-bag rate, share of Christmas-week itineraries, and repeat-customer rate. If unavailable, label all assumed proportions and run sensitivity cases (for example, more family visitors, more vacationers, or fewer agency passengers). Avoid using age, nationality, or income to guess trip behavior when the travel constraints are already known.
