# Kestrel Air: memo to the board (Team 09)

## What we decided

| | When, for whom | Actions | Cost |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Week 4 (Thanksgiving, during the engine inspections), all customers | Lease 1 aircraft with crew for two weeks · extra airport and call-center staff · free changes and cancellations Nov 23–Dec 6 | $1.05M |
| 2 | Week 8 (strike week), all customers | Backup ground handling at both bases on Dec 22–23 · partner-airline rebooking for weeks 8–9 · free changes and cancellations Dec 21–Jan 3 | $0.95M |
| | | **Both decisions** | **$2.00M of $3M** |

**Why.** The grounding took 3 of 36 aircraft out over Thanksgiving, and the lease replaces one of them. The strike hits the busiest days of the year. Backup crews keep the bases turning aircraft around, and the partner deal gets a stranded family home the same day instead of three days later. Free changes until Jan 3 protect travelers if the union extends the strike.

## What the simulation says

66 customer personas stand for 2.17 million trips over the ten weeks. Each week they react to what happens to their flights, our messages, the news and their friends' posts. Two seeds per scenario; these are simulated results, not forecasts.

| Board goal | Target | Our plan | No decision |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 Flights operated (strike days left out) | ≥98% | 96.9% (missed) | 95.1% |
| 2 Disrupted passengers rebooked or refunded within 24h | ≥90% | 100% | 95–100% |
| 3 Complaints | no worse than last winter* | a third to a half fewer than no decision (20–21 vs 29–43 per 660 customer-weeks) | — |
| 3b NPS (+12 today) | no worse than last winter* | +8 to +14 | −20 to −8 |
| 4 Revenue, weeks 5–10 | ≥96% of plan | 98–100% | 94–100% |
| 5 Budget | ≤$3M | $2.00M | $0 |

\* Last winter's figures are not given; we use today's (2,500 complaints a month, NPS +12).

The plan does better in both seeds on everything customers decide. In week 10, trust is 3.5–3.7 out of 5 against 2.9–3.1, and 59–69% would book Kestrel again against 34–62%. Customers' willingness to recommend ends near where it started; with no decision it falls by 20–32 points. Without decisions, strike-day families, including one traveling with an elderly parent, cancel their trips. On the strike days, cancellations among our customers' 16 flights fall from 3 to 0–1.

## What it cannot tell

- **Goal 1 depends on how many flights the grounding really cancels.** Under our reading the plan operates 96.9%; under milder readings of the grounding it reaches 97–98.4%. We would not promise 98%.
- **Goal 2 assumes the partner airline has free seats** in a week that is 97% booked.
- **Sizes.** Simulated customers get the direction right more often than the size. Without the surprises the plan pushes NPS to +48, which real customers would not do in a strike week.
- **Complaint counts are not calibrated to Kestrel's data**, so we report the direction only.
- **Not simulated:** a strike extension, or controllers calling in sick once the Dec 30 paycheck is missed.

## What the company should watch next

1. **Partner seats for Dec 21–Jan 3.** Confirm capacity in writing; Goal 2 rests on it.
2. **The union talks.** The backup contract covers Dec 22–23 only. If the strike is extended, extend backup ground handling with it.
3. **The shutdown.** The House votes after Christmas, and federal workers may miss their Dec 30 paycheck; the FAA says it may slow traffic at busy airports if more controllers call in sick. In 2025 the FAA ordered flight cuts at 40 airports five weeks into the shutdown. Watch New Year week, and plan for capacity cuts if it lasts into January.
4. **The week-4 message.** It said no Kestrel aircraft had a problem with the part, and the update the same week found worn parts on three. Correct the record with the customers who received it.
5. **January complaints and NPS by customer group**, against these simulated directions, to calibrate the next simulation.
